# Using the intelligence desk

The opening uses normal document scrolling: no pinned story panel, scroll-linked image zoom, or viewport-height story chapters. The three-part summary is compact; topic cards show up to two reading leads with an Explore link for the full topic. Brief opacity-only fades respect reduced-motion settings. The progress line and back-to-top control remain available.

The story-led opening includes a compact **Since your last visit** strip based on this browser's saved collection baseline. First visits establish a baseline instead of labeling everything new; disabled storage is disclosed. Pending followed-development changes open the existing inbox and are not necessarily changes since the last visit. Nothing is fetched or generated by AI for this summary.

Below the shortlist, four topic chapters highlight Quantum, Cybersecurity/PQC, AI, and Government coverage. Their counts reflect matching collected stories, not exhaustive field activity. Headlines open story details; Explore narrows the feed. Topic chapters step aside while filters are active. Publisher images retain their aspect ratio and attribution. Missing or blocked images use a text-first layout; no unrelated stock artwork is substituted.

The home page opens **Latest**, with one lead story, a compact shortlist of up to three other developments, and a news list below. Stories come before controls. Open **Browse, filter & follow** for topics, search, ordering, following, and coverage details. The page uses the collected report window; it does not perform a live news search. Quantum computing and cybersecurity/PQC are core coverage; AI and cloud are adjacent coverage.

**Developments first** is the default order. The lead and shortlist require an explicit development with a valid publication date within seven days of the report edition. Guides, commentary, personnel updates, and undated items stay in the collection without being promoted as fresh developments. Recency, reported stage, source perspective, and variety guide selection. **Why this leads** exposes the evidence and limitations. A source-reported deployment is not independent proof of delivery. A weak edition is labeled **From the collection**, rather than given a manufactured lead.

**What changed along the way** appears only when collected evidence supports multiple dated observations about the same specific project, mission, or document identifier, with at least two reported stages. Shared broad topics or company names are insufficient. Dates belong to publications, not necessarily the events. Expand **Evidence & limits** for each observation's exact source statement. No timeline means insufficient qualifying evidence, not that nothing happened.

Search matches collected titles, source excerpts, and source names. **Recently collected** orders by report date and existing relevance within a date; ties retain collection order. **Frontier Signal relevance** puts the existing rule-based decision priority first. Neither order is a quality rating. Source publication dates are shown separately when available. These alternative orders reflect your choice rather than an editorial endorsement.

**Story details** opens an accessible briefing with collected source excerpts, potential impact labeled as rule-based interpretation, uncertainty, original evidence, and related reporting. Connections to organizations, missions, funding, patents, and standards show their matching basis. An exact evidence URL differs from contextual matching by a named organization, mission, identifier, or technology. Context does not prove a partnership, patent implementation, compliance, or independent confirmation. Missing links remain missing rather than being invented. Opening a briefing or original source does not mark it read automatically.

Interpretation centers on the particular claim: the reported mechanism, measured result, named deliverable, agreement, or access change. Each briefing pairs a concise explanation with one focused follow-up question instead of repeating a broad topic checklist. A grant mentioned in a deployment story does not turn the deployment into an open solicitation. Expand **Source passages behind this interpretation** to inspect the evidence used; a passage supporting multiple observations appears once. Story-specific open issues appear once, while common provenance and collection caveats stay under **How this briefing was assembled**. Thin excerpts receive a short limitation rather than an invented impact. These are deterministic evidence-linked checks, not independent technical validation, AI-generated analysis, or personalization from private reader data.

When multiple publishers cover the same identifiable development, Latest can show a single card labeled with its source-account count. **Compare source accounts** keeps each publisher's title, dates, excerpt, quantities, and perspective separate. The grouping requires specific shared event evidence and compatible dates/scope, not merely a common organization or topic. It is a reading aid, not a vote or proof of corroboration. Search also matches grouped accounts. Saved readings and read markers remain attached to their original source IDs.

**Permanent briefing** opens a source-specific page with its own stable address; **Copy link** shares that briefing rather than just the publisher URL. The **story archive** searches older briefings beyond the rolling homepage window. Publication dates, first/last report observations, and changes in captured titles or excerpts are labeled separately. Repeated appearances in a digest do not become a new publication date, and a changed excerpt does not prove that the publisher edited its article. Public captured evidence is retained before daily-report pruning; private notes and reader preferences are never part of that archive. Archived interpretation uses current Frontier Signal rules and remains labeled as interpretation.

**Inside this collection** shows topic counts and selection exclusions. The offline `news_intelligence` layer removes routine award/opportunity records, off-topic matches, publication dates after the edition or more than 30 days before it, and explicitly labeled replay/past-event pages. Unknown dates remain visible and labeled. Canonical URL duplicates and conservatively matched near-identical same-date headlines are grouped; distinct preprints are not merged by title similarity. These are automated rules, not human editorial review. The original reading brief, report archive, and Federal landscape are unchanged. Rebuilding the site does not refresh collection.

Source concentration is visible in the coverage disclosure. The [source variety pass](NEWS-SOURCES.md) adds first-party research, standards-community updates, security assessments, and maintainer releases to future collection. More publishers do not necessarily mean independent evidence; the current archive is not retroactively diversified.

**From collection to the homepage** distinguishes source accounts present in the report window, retained accounts, grouped story cards, and featured cards. It also separates AI/cloud accounts with quantum overlap from accounts without that overlap. The latest recorded collector check is a different time window, so these counts are not a conversion funnel. An enabled source without a recorded check is labeled **No recorded check**, not failed. New sources are measured when scheduled collection runs; rebuilding the dashboard does not collect new stories.

**Customize your feed** follows topics and organizations named in this collection. **Following** matches either a followed topic or organization, with other visible filters still applied. **Mark read**, **Mark unread**, and **Unread only** are explicit and separate from notebook saving and Briefing read state. **New since last visit** compares newly seen source IDs against the previous Latest visit and report-date baseline. It excludes older backfills and retained items; it does not claim a story was newly published. The first visit sets a baseline without labeling everything new. Visiting another tool alone does not consume that baseline. Preferences are stored under `quantum-scout:news-preferences:v1`, with limits of 80 organizations, 1,500 read IDs, and 4,000 known IDs. They stay on this browser and origin, without notifications, account sync, or inclusion in notebook exports. Storage failure leaves page-only controls with a warning. Topic/search/order/feed filters themselves are session-only.

**Read original** opens the source. **Save** uses the same browser-local notebook as the rest of the site. Story images are the original article's publisher-selected previews, credited to the publisher. Images load directly from the publisher's image host without a referrer, though that host still receives the visitor's IP address. Missing, blocked, and repeated previews become clean text-only cards. A publisher preview can itself be an illustration; it is not proof of the event. Missing excerpts stay explicit, and the collection warning remains visible when data is old, undated, or future-dated. **More stories** adds six rows without repeating featured items.

Publisher previews are collected separately with `python scripts/enrich_story_images.py --reports reports --max-items 36`. The bounded daily step reads article Open Graph or Twitter Card image metadata and records its provenance in `reports/story-images.json`; it does not download or rehost image files. Successful lookups are cached for 30 days and missing previews for one day. Site builds only read that cache and never need network access. A failed preview lookup cannot prevent a report or the site from being published. Check the original publisher's terms before republishing its images beyond this linked preview use.

Use **Light mode** in the top bar for an off-white reading surface, or **Dark mode** to return to the navy palette. Dark is the default. The choice is stored only in this browser and carries across the desk, organization profiles, reloads, and other open tabs on the same origin. It does not change your saved items or notes. If browser storage is blocked, switching still works for the current page. Printed pages retain the existing light print styling.

The top navigation offers Latest, Briefing, Tech landscape, Federal, Patents, and Saved. **Tools** retains Question Lab, Investigations, Review desk, Report library, Getting started, and Sources & methods. On smaller screens, **Menu** opens navigation; Escape closes the Tools disclosure first if open, then the menu. Existing deep links still work. Getting started retains its topic-to-Question-Lab handoff without making an AI request.

Choose **Technology briefing** for the default **Decision briefing** covering cybersecurity/PQC, quantum computing, AI, and cloud. It shows what the source reports, what the development could affect, a suggested next step, and suggested review timing. Open a headline to read the evidence. **Research reading** keeps the paper-first workflow, source appraisal, and Question Lab handoff available.

## Keep a living investigation

Save a question in **Question Lab**, then open **Investigations**. This uses the same saved record, not a second copy. Your hypothesis, study plan, feasibility notes, next step, and attached sources appear together. Evidence roles such as Supports and Challenges are your classifications, not independently verified relationships; no challenging source does not imply consensus.

The prior-work matrix keeps your notes about each attached source's method, data, assumptions, metrics, and limitations, with optional research-question and findings columns. Missing fields stay explicitly unrecorded. Use **Compare attached excerpts with AI** to stage the attached readings in the notebook; preview the outbound excerpts and consent before any request. After comparison, choose an investigation and approve individual proposed entries. Only sources already attached to that investigation are eligible. Nothing is automatically selected or saved, and replacing a different existing entry requires separate permission. Review original papers before treating any comparison as a finding.

Each approved entry retains its exact selected passages, original excerpt, source URL/title, provider/model, format version, and approval time. Expand **Approved source passages** in the matrix to inspect this context. Approval records your classification decision, not independent verification. Editing a cell later keeps its original trace and marks it **Edited since approval**. Unsaved drafts, changed evidence, conflicting browser-tab writes, or storage failures block approval without discarding the review selections.

Related developments are matched locally against the available reading snapshot, with the matching terms and source/report dates shown. They exclude already-attached sources. These are reading leads, not exhaustive literature results or verified relevance. Any since-review indicator describes the saved local review baseline, not a claim that a paper was published yesterday. This feature does not subscribe you to notifications or run a background search.

Investigation notes and comparison fields are included in **Export lab backup** and restored with the question. Old backups remain readable. Notebook backups are still separate. Nothing syncs across browsers or devices, and private notes are not automatically sent to AI.

## A decision-focused start

The shortlist contains one lead and up to two other developments, followed by more matches without duplicate cards. **Why prioritized & what to verify** exposes the topic and signal cues, provenance, limits, and ranking basis. These are rule-based suggestions from collected titles and excerpts, not AI-generated analysis, verified impacts, full-document review, or personalized advice.

Decision relevance favors dated, relevant official policy/security signals, then releases, evaluations and potentially relevant opportunity context, then research, roadmaps and background. Publication dates that are unavailable, future-dated relative to the edition, or more than 30 days old reduce priority and require a freshness check. Corporate financing is not treated as a technical result, and USAspending awards are treated as past-award context rather than open solicitations. Rank ties use report date and a stable identifier, not claimed precision about importance. Original report scores remain unchanged.

**Review timing** means when it may be useful to examine a signal, not when a technology will mature, a binding deadline, or an instruction to deploy. Stale collection replaces this timing with a freshness warning. Draft/proposed policy is not presented as a settled obligation; research attacks are not operational incidents. Verify scope and current evidence before taking action.

The new default decision view does not overwrite existing research interests or ordering. Each mode keeps those preferences separately; date-window, source-type and unread filters are shared and visible. Saved readings, notes, read state and questions are unchanged. This view does not send anything to an AI service. If decision metadata is missing from an older snapshot, assessment is shown as unavailable instead of inferred in the browser.

## Research reading: a focused routine

The research view starts with a **three-item reading plan**: one lead source and up to two more in your selected order. Each explains its selection through the existing relevance rules and matched terms, not an AI endorsement or academic quality score. The shortlist respects the same interest, source-type, date-window, sort and unread controls as the rest of the feed; sources are not duplicated below it. Save and mark-read actions retain their existing browser-local behavior.

**Added source links** compares unique URLs in the latest report with the previous available report, even when dates are not consecutive. It counts all topics before related-coverage grouping. Added does not mean newly published, never seen before, or new to the field. Without two reports the comparison is unavailable, not zero. Publication dates remain separate from report dates.

**Changes to examine** surfaces recorded claim changes, conflicts and supersessions separately from added links. These are snapshot records across tracked themes, not live literature monitoring or verified resolutions. Collection counts have moved into an expandable panel. **Explore this topic in Question Lab** loads the lead title as a starting interest and clears provider consent; it does not attach evidence, generate questions, or make an AI call.

1. Check the edition date and collection-health indicator. A fresh site build does not mean fresh collection.
2. Start with **Past 7 days**, the default for new browsers, or choose **Latest edition** for that report alone. The seven-day window is anchored to the latest available report, not the site build or today's date. New browsers start with **Cyber / PQC + quantum** and research-first ordering; existing preferences are retained. A sparse shortlist offers **Browse core research · past 7 days**, which explicitly restores the core-topic, all-source-types, research-first week while retaining the unread setting. Narrow to either discipline or use **All sources**, **AI & cloud**, or **Government**. The source-type selector distinguishes known preprint repositories, government documents, industry sources, and other/unverified types. On a phone, expand the reading controls to customize this view.
3. Read the source excerpt, then open the original for its full assumptions and caveats. Excerpts can be truncated in the underlying report.
4. Choose **Mark read** when you are finished; opening a source link does not mark it read. **Unread only** narrows the list, and the progress count reflects the chosen topic and reading window. You can mark an item unread again.
5. Save useful items. Saved copies remain in your browser even after they leave the current reading window. Their publication and report dates remain attached; saving does not keep a source current.
6. Use the horizon panel for dated opportunities and the review desk for changes that need investigation.

Use **Aa** in the top bar for larger reading text. The desk remembers your text preference, topic, reading window, and unread filter locally.

The visual language is **1950s Atompunk**: warm ivory, midnight navy, oxidized teal, and signal orange; geometric display headings, monospaced instrument labels, squared panels, and technical-grid margins. Reading cards stay opaque, body text stays in a conventional sans-serif, and decorative geometry does not clip controls or focus outlines. System fonts require no external font service. The same palette carries through the report library and organization profiles, with reduced-motion and print styles retained.

## Beyond the headline and following through

Relevant **Decision briefing** cards now include **Beyond the headline**. Expand it for the attached, attributed source excerpt (or a clearly labeled headline when no excerpt was collected), three review questions tailored to the signal type, the limits of that evidence, and a suggested next check. Policy, security, research, release, evaluation, roadmap, and business signals get different prompts. These are rules, not an AI fact-check or a full-document assessment. The interface does not claim that a benchmark, independent evaluation, or contrary evidence is absent merely because it was not attached.

Below the shortlist, **Whatever happened to…?** provides archive follow-ups independent of the reading filters. Its date is the latest report edition, not today's browser clock or the site rebuild time. Expand a card's timeline to inspect the original announcement/target and any stored updates, with each time role labeled.

The initial review queue uses two bounded sources:

- **Mission registry:** one most recently elapsed exact-date target per mission that remains configured as planned or monitoring. Targets on the edition date or later, approximate dates, completed, cancelled, and superseded milestones are excluded. Accepted or curated program updates are context, never automatic milestone resolution.
- **Historical evidence archive:** high-confidence published announcements, pilots, or roadmaps 30–730 days old with a tracked-technology cue. Thirty days is an editorial review threshold, not a promised delivery date. At least three non-generic title words must overlap before a later archived/current reading is offered as possible related coverage. Such overlap does not establish the same project, independent replication, or progress. Unknown discovery dates are not substituted for publication dates.

The queue alternates mission and archive leads, includes at most 12 cards, and initially displays two. Excluded or missing evidence does not imply success or failure. This is snapshot review, not a live web search; confirm freshness and read original sources before deciding what happened.

**Follow locally** is a bookmark in this browser, separate from notebook storage. Use **Followed only** to narrow the displayed queue. It enables no notifications, background service, or cross-device sync. Followed items can leave the bounded snapshot without being resolved. Storage errors are shown, and unreadable stored data is not overwritten. These IDs are not included in notebook exports.

Search titles, assignees, identifiers or tags, filter the recorded case stage, and sort by research relevance, latest recorded publication/grant date, or collector score. Research relevance prioritizes those domains in the order above; it does not assess novelty or scientific quality. Latest-event ordering uses the later valid publication/grant date, not the collection date.

Counts describe the actual dashboard records, not a larger upstream family ledger. Expand the inspector's family section to see recorded member links, parent/child references and the grouping basis. The inspector separates publication identifiers, grant numbers, filing/publication/grant dates and source-reported case status. An application publication identifier can coexist with a later grant in the same case. Records containing only applicant/publication metadata are labeled **Technical text not collected**, not displayed as abstracts. Summary text and curated assessments are not verified claim interpretations. Missing or zero citation data reads **No citations recorded**, not proof of no citations. **Related reading leads** finds shared technical phrases in patent titles/available summaries and the public Frontier Signal reading window. It does not use private notebook notes or make a network/AI request. The reading checklist is explicitly a set of prompts, not extracted findings.

## Find and keep useful evidence

Research titles and excerpts typeset `$…$`, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, and `\[…\]` mathematics with locally bundled KaTeX, including fractions, superscripts, and accents. Dynamic search results and profile evidence use the same renderer. MathML is included for assistive technology. Long expressions scroll within their own region on narrow screens; titles and search excerpts are not line-clamped. Unsupported notation remains visible as the original text. Saved data, personal notes, and exports retain their original notation. This does not restore text already shortened by an upstream report; open the original paper for the full abstract and proof.

Press **/** to search readings, patents, tracked organizations and technologies, missions, and funding records. Results are grouped by type, with title matches ranked first. Choose a type tab to narrow the search and **Show more results** to continue browsing. **Escape** closes search. This searches the published snapshot and your saved reading copies, not the web or every historical report.

In **Research notebook**, expand **Your research notes** on a saved source to record a research question, critical appraisal, and next step. These are your personal notes, not extracted findings. Record section/page references when evaluating claims, and distinguish a source's statements from your interpretation. Notes save locally as you type; a visible warning indicates storage failure. Removing an annotated source asks for confirmation because it also removes its notes.

Search the notebook by title, source, topic, or note text. **Export notebook** downloads a version-2 JSON copy of all saved source excerpts, links, dates, read status, citation metadata, and notes, not just filtered results. **Export citations (.bib)** includes repository-supplied authors, publication year, DOI, and repository name when available, plus provenance and version caveats. Sources without retrieved metadata remain minimal source-link records. All entries remain `@misc`: a repository-reported venue is not independently confirmed publication status. Review the version and missing fields before citing.

### Import references from your library (RIS)

In **Research notebook**, choose **Import references or backup** and select a ProQuest or citation-manager RIS export. This single control detects RIS references or Frontier Signal JSON backups from the file contents, not the extension. The RIS preview lists every record, abstract, missing fields, and duplicates. Select individual records or **Select all new**, then **Save selected references**. Cancel makes no changes. The parser runs entirely in your browser: no credentials, upload, external lookup, or AI call is required. RIS imports also work on the public site without signing into the AI service.

The importer supports UTF-8 RIS (5 MB / 500 records per file). It retains titles, authors, abstracts, export-reported publication years, venue, keywords, DOI, database, accession ID, source links, and import time. arXiv links are preferred when supplied; otherwise it uses a supplied DOI or usable source link. Library/session parameters are removed from ProQuest links, and unsafe or session-bearing links are omitted. No full papers are downloaded. Missing fields stay unknown, dates are not inferred from export timestamps, and journal/preprint labels are not proof of peer review. DOIs in exports can identify associated material or another version: verify at the original source.

Exact DOI, arXiv ID (across URL/version variations), ProQuest document ID, or normalized source-link matches are flagged as duplicates. Titles alone never establish a duplicate. Existing readings and notes win; import does not silently enrich or replace them. New items start as **To review**, not **Reviewed**. Each saved reference shows **RIS metadata & source links**, retained in notebook JSON backups. Imported metadata remains unverified; the existing BibTeX exporter does not elevate it to independently retrieved source metadata.

To connect a reference, open/create a question in **Question Lab**, choose it from **Attach a saved reading**, choose its evidence role, and click **Attach evidence**. The abstract and bibliographic fields travel with that attachment. Private notes are not copied into AI context. To request AI assistance, explicitly select the reading and consent to sharing its excerpt; check the source's terms before sharing licensed text. Importing never sends anything to an AI provider automatically.

Browser storage is finite (the importer caps the resulting notebook at 1,000 readings / 4 MB). A failed write leaves the notebook unchanged. A changed notebook in another tab requires a reload before importing. Export a backup after import; public-site and local-lab storage are separate.

### Restore a notebook backup

Choose **Import references or backup** and select a Frontier Signal JSON export (current v2 or older v1 reading-list format). The preview lists new readings, existing-source conflicts, and duplicates. Nothing changes until **Merge new readings** is selected. Existing source copies and notes always win; imports restore notes and read status only for new URLs. The first occurrence wins when a URL appears repeatedly within a file. Keep the original backup to retain skipped entries. Imports do not restore your filters or the separate analyst-action history.

The entire file is validated before merging (5 MB, 1,000 readings; note-field limits match the editor). Invalid structure, oversized notes, or unsafe source links reject the whole file. An atomic browser-storage write must succeed before the notebook changes; a full or blocked store leaves existing data untouched. Cancel is safe. Imported citation metadata is not treated as verified: it must match independently retrieved site metadata before enriched citation export. There is no automatic device sync; export and import manually when moving browsers or origins.

### Compare saved papers

Expand **Compare saved papers**, select two to four saved readings with abstracts or excerpts, and choose Gemini or the Groq backup. Expand the payload preview to inspect the exact titles, source links, and excerpts (up to 2,500 characters each) that will be sent. Confirm consent, then choose **Compare selected papers**. Private notes, annotations, and unselected readings are excluded. Check license/provider terms before sharing library abstracts; importing alone never calls AI.

The app numbers each source excerpt into sentences such as `S1.T1`. The AI selects up to three sentence IDs per aspect instead of copying quotations. The app inserts the original text, preserving numbers, punctuation, negation, and internal whitespace, and displays each selected sentence separately. Invalid, cross-source, repeated, or out-of-order IDs are withheld with a warning. Expand **Numbered source excerpt** to review every sentence in context. The browser also checks that the returned catalog covers the supplied excerpt without altering its text and that selected IDs resolve to the displayed passages.

Categories are **AI-proposed, not verified**: original wording does not prove that the selected passage supports the category or interpretation. The current format covers the study's aim, method, data/study material, explicit assumptions, measures/metrics, findings, and limitations. Metrics must be measures actually used, and assumptions must be stated rather than inferred. **Explicit study limitations** should describe an explicit constraint of that study, not general adoption barriers, prior API-usability problems, or background concerns. An empty limitations cell is valid and does not establish that the full paper lacks limitations. Review the full paper before relying on these classifications.

Sentence splitting is deterministic but heuristic, accounting for common abbreviations and decimal numbers. Long sentences are retained rather than truncated; at the 80-entry limit, the remaining excerpt stays together in the last entry. IDs belong to this comparison only and are not permanent citation identifiers.

Cross-paper interpretations cite sentence IDs from each source, with the app inserting the original supporting text. Numerical restatements in connections are conservatively withheld; read quantities in the original source sentences. **Shared topics** appear separately from **AI-proposed agreement**, which remains unverified. Obvious “both discuss…” topic-overlap claims are relabeled, but this rule cannot validate every comparison. No connections is a valid result, not proof that none exist. The follow-up section offers questions to check in the full papers. This is not full-text review, contradiction detection, independent verification, or proof of novelty.

Comparison uses one call from the same local or hosted usage budget as other lab assistance. Format `comparison_evidence_version: 4` enables all seven categories; version 3 remains compatible with aim, method, findings, and limitations only. Earlier backends are stopped before any comparison request is sent. Restart the private lab after updating code; a hosted Worker must be deployed separately. Deploying dashboard assets alone does not update the endpoint. Failed requests can still consume a slot. There is no automatic retry or provider switch. On a Gemini availability error, **Choose Groq backup** only changes the provider and clears consent: review the excerpts, consent again, and click **Compare selected papers** to send.

Use **Review once. Keep the useful evidence.** to approve selected entries into an existing investigation. No additional AI call is needed to save. Empty or withheld categories cannot be approved and never erase existing work. Unapproved suggestions remain only in the current tab and clear on reload or a changed selection. Approved entries and source traces persist in browser-local question storage and **Export lab backup**; the separate notebook readings and private annotations are unchanged. A changed target clears all selections and replacement consent.

### Source-backed citations

Expand **Citation metadata** beneath a reading. **Paper metadata** includes repository-supplied authors, date, DOI, venue and version where supplied. **Article metadata** includes source-reported authors, publisher, date and a canonical source link from HTML meta tags or article JSON-LD. The resolver supports arXiv/ePrint and selected public news, industry and government publishers (see `ARTICLE_HOSTS` in `citations.py`). **Not checked** means no cached lookup; **Lookup failed** identifies an actual unsuccessful attempt. Missing fields remain unknown, and a year-only date stays year-only. Linked arXiv/ePrint papers and DOI records appear as separate source links: their authors and dates are never assigned to the covering article, and the relationship is not independently verified. This is link discovery, not DOI/publisher metadata resolution. A DOI or reported venue is not proof of peer review; that status remains unverified. Article metadata is included in notebook citation exports.

Citation metadata is separate from your frozen saved excerpt and can be refreshed from the public cache. Check that it describes the version you read. A failed refresh retains the last good record, its original retrieval date, and a warning. Offline builds use the checked-in cache; neither the build nor your browser contacts scholarly services.

## Scholarly evidence and limitations

Source-type labels describe provenance, not scientific quality. arXiv abstract/PDF links and numbered IACR ePrint records are labeled **Preprint repository**; later publication and peer review remain unverified. Government domains identify provenance, not document type or correctness. Industry attribution uses the existing recorded source-name classification. All other sources remain **Other / unverified type**, which can include genuine scholarly publications; the preprint filter is not a complete literature search. Related coverage is not grouped across these source types.

Critical reading prompts are deterministic guidance, not paper-specific extracted methods or limitations. Cyber/PQC prompts emphasize threat models, security assumptions, parameter sets, and baselines. Quantum prompts emphasize assumptions, simulation versus hardware, resource overhead, and reproducibility. This desk does not establish novelty, exhaustively search the literature, or automatically identify research gaps. Report scores remain monitoring priorities, not academic quality or citation-impact ratings.

## The workspaces

- **Federal landscape:** the full public mission portfolio, funding records, opportunity radar, procurement documents, contractors, and relationship evidence.
- **Patent watch:** search by title, assignee, or strategic domain; sort by strategic significance or publication/grant date; use **Show more patents** to browse the portfolio.

## Reading order and freshness

The available reading window spans seven calendar days ending on the latest valid digest. **Latest edition** shows items from that report; **Past 7 days** includes the wider window. Coverage counts reflect actual available reports and distinct source links, not assumed daily collection. **Research first** orders core-topic preprints, core official evidence, technical coverage, broad matches/funding context, then other context. Explicit technical matches and the relevance tier appear on each card. Ties use report date and then the unchanged operational score. A publication's repository location does not establish quality. **Government priority** restores official-source-first ordering within each report day; **Latest report first** emphasizes chronology.

Matching uses titles and source excerpts, not report categories. Recipient names, award amounts/IDs, and collector search labels do not establish research relevance. Procurement records remain supporting context even when they match a technical term. Interest filters narrow the set without changing original scores or removing records from the federal workspace. Authoritative `.gov` and `.mil` sources remain in **Government** even without government keywords. There is no per-source cap that can displace a higher-tier paper.

Publication dates are validated. Missing or malformed dates remain unavailable; report dates and discovery dates are not substituted as publication dates. Malformed source URLs and links with embedded username/password credentials are excluded from the reading brief; sensitive query values are redacted.

Related coverage is grouped only when publication dates match and headlines have substantial keyword overlap. Distinct preprint URLs are never collapsed by headline similarity. This is a reading convenience, not corroboration or an assertion that the articles describe precisely the same event. Every grouped source link remains available. Undated stories are not grouped by headline similarity.

The research pulse measures linked evidence volume across the last fourteen recorded days. It is not a confidence score or a forecast, and missing collection days are not invented as zero observations.

Three clocks remain separate:

| Clock | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Edition | The latest daily report available to the site |
| Collection | The latest recorded collector observation; older than 36 hours prompts a freshness warning |
| Site build | When the static site was assembled; does not reset evidence age |

Passed mission dates are labeled as recorded milestones requiring verification. Completion is never inferred from the calendar alone. Deadlines shown on the homepage exclude dates that have already passed in America/Chicago, but the source remains the authority for cutoff times and amendments.
